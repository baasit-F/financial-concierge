Living benefits

Life insurance isn't just for when you die. It's living insurance.

More people than ever survive heart attacks, strokes and cancer, but recovery can mean months of lost income and medical bills. A lot of life insurance policies include or offer living benefits: the option to use part of your policy's value while you're still alive. Living benefits can help cover medical bills, unexpected financial hardship, ongoing treatment and care, and your family's security. Living benefits act like a bridge between leaving a legacy behind and your retirement plans today.

Critical illness

Diagnosed with a qualifying condition such as a heart attack, stroke or cancer? You may be able to access a portion of your benefit to pay for treatment, travel or the mortgage while you recover.

Chronic illness

If you can no longer perform two of the six activities of daily living (bathing, dressing, eating, toileting, transferring, continence), benefits can help pay for in-home care or assisted living.

Terminal illness

With a terminal diagnosis, you may be able to receive much of your benefit early, so you can focus on family, settle affairs and make the most of the time you have.

Long-term care rider

Use part of your benefit for care at home, in assisted living or in a nursing facility, including memory care, the kind of costs health insurance usually won't cover.

Return of premium

Worried you'll "never use it"? With a return-of-premium rider, you can get some or all of your premiums back if you outlive your term or no longer need the coverage, depending on the policy.

Cash value

Permanent policies can build cash value over time that you can borrow against for opportunities or emergencies. Loans and withdrawals reduce the death benefit.

Traditional thinking

  • Pays only after you pass away
  • A serious illness can drain savings first
  • "I'll never use it"

Living insurance

  • Protects your family if you pass away
  • May help pay the bills if you get seriously ill
  • Money you can use however you need
  1. 1
    A qualifying diagnosis

    A critical, chronic or terminal illness that meets the policy's definitions.

  2. 2
    You request a benefit

    You file a claim to accelerate part of your death benefit.

  3. 3
    You decide how to use it

    Medical bills, the mortgage, lost income, care. What's left goes to your beneficiaries.

Good to know

  • If you never need your living benefits, your beneficiaries receive the full death benefit.
  • Some riders are built in at no extra cost. Others add to your premium.
  • Using a living benefit reduces the amount paid to your beneficiaries.
  • A doctor must certify the diagnosis, and some riders have a waiting period (often 90 days for care benefits).

Long-term care

Three ways to plan for long-term care.

People are living longer, care costs keep rising and families are more spread out than ever. A plan made today protects your savings, your independence and your choice of care. As a Certified in Long-Term Care (CLTC) professional, Ben helps you pick the right fit.

Traditional LTC insurance

A stand-alone policy dedicated to care at home, in assisted living or in a nursing facility, up to a set benefit limit.

Best for: the most care coverage per premium dollar. No cash value or death benefit.

Popular

Hybrid (asset-based) LTC

Life insurance or an annuity with long-term care benefits built in. If you need care, it pays for care. If you never do, it still pays a benefit to your family.

Best for: people who don't want to "use it or lose it." Many designs offer premiums that never increase, single or joint coverage, and one-time or ongoing payments.

LTC rider on life insurance

An add-on that lets you use part of your death benefit for care if you become chronically ill or cognitively impaired. Anything you don't use goes to your beneficiaries.

Best for: adding care protection to coverage you already need. It adds to your premium and reduces the death benefit when used.

Quick answers

Does it cost anything to get a quote?

No. Quotes and consultations are 100% free with no obligation.

How much life insurance do I need?

A common starting point is 10–15× your income, but debts, kids and goals all matter. Try the calculator above, then Ben will fine-tune it with you.

Term or permanent, which is better?

They solve different problems. Term is budget-friendly protection for a set period. Permanent lasts your whole life and may build cash value. Many families use both.

Can I switch my term policy to permanent later?

Often, yes. Many term policies include a conversion option that lets you switch to permanent coverage without a new medical exam, as long as you convert within the policy's conversion period. That can be valuable if your health changes.

Can a small business offer group benefits?

Yes. Many carriers offer group plans for businesses with as few as two employees.

What are living benefits?

Living benefits let you use part of your life insurance death benefit early if you're diagnosed with a qualifying critical, chronic or terminal illness. What's available depends on the policy, carrier and state. See how they work.

Do I need a medical exam?

Not always. Many carriers now offer policies with simplified issue or no-exam underwriting.

What if I never use my living benefits?

Then your beneficiaries receive the full death benefit. With a return-of-premium rider, you may also get premiums back if you outlive your term coverage.
